Special Roads

While driving on public roads you will come across different types of roads. Some are wider and others narrower. In addition, you can and may drive much faster on one road than on the other. Adapted rules apply on a number of roads.

The main special roads with adapted rules are:

Motorways and Autowegen

🛣️
Motorway
130 km/h
Min. required speed: 60 km/h capability

If no other speed is specified. Driving much slower than traffic is dangerous.

🚗
Autoweg
100 km/h
Min. required speed: 50 km/h capability

If no other speed is specified. Only motor vehicles that can and may reach this speed.

  • Only motor vehicles that can and may drive at least 50 km/h are allowed to use the autoweg.
  • Only motor vehicles that can and may drive at least 60 km/h are allowed to use the motorway.

💡 You may not ride a moped on an autoweg or motorway — even if it has been upgraded to reach 50 or 60 km/h — because mopeds have a legal speed limit of 45 km/h. Likewise, if a passenger car cannot exceed 40 km/h due to technical problems, it may not enter, even if it is normally permitted to drive faster.

ℹ There is no minimum required speed on an autoweg or motorway itself. However, driving at 60 km/h on a quiet motorway where others maintain 80–130 km/h is dangerous and prohibited under Article 5 of the Road Traffic Act.

A number of rules apply to keep motorways and autowegen safe. You may not:

🚫 Prohibited on motorway / autoweg
  • Make a U-turn or reverse
  • Stop on the carriageway (involuntary stopping due to traffic is allowed)
  • Drive or wait on the hard shoulder, emergency refuge areas, or in the verge — except in emergencies

On the motorway, a vehicle combination (e.g. passenger car with trailer) of more than 7 metres in length may only drive in the two rightmost lanes — unless a different lane is needed to pre-sort. This rule also applies to lorry drivers.

Rush-Hour Lane

On the motorway you will regularly encounter rush-hour lanes — either an extra lane on the left side of the carriageway, or the hard shoulder used as an extra lane. Signs next to the road and matrix signs above lanes indicate whether the rush-hour lane is open.

⚠ If you see a red cross above a lane, you are not allowed to drive on that lane. Also pay attention to the maximum speed limit — it can change as soon as the rush-hour lane opens.

Erf

It is often thought that pedestrians have right of way on an erf. This is not the case. Within the erf, the same priority rules apply as on normal roads. The entrances and exits are usually designed as an entrance or exit construction — when entering or exiting, drivers must give way to all other traffic, including pedestrians.

There are a few other rules within an erf:

  • Pedestrians are allowed to use the road across the entire width — they do not have to walk on the side of the road, as there are no pavements within an erf.
  • The speed limit for drivers is 15 km/h.
  • Drivers of a motor vehicle may only park in places designated as parking places (marked by a P sign or P on the road surface). Parking outside these places is not allowed.
