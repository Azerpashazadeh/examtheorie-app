Driving Behaviour at Intersections

Different rules apply for when you encounter other traffic at intersections. It is important to understand the difference between road users (all traffic, including pedestrians) and drivers (all traffic without pedestrians). In addition, understand the difference between traffic on the intersecting road and traffic on the same road.

Intersection

Intersection or junction of roads. At an intersection it is possible to change direction — you can turn left or right. Intersections can be designed in many different ways.

Intersecting Road

A road that crosses the road you are on. This does not always have to be at 90 degrees — it can also be oblique, such as at a Y-junction.

The Same Road

The road you are on. Traffic on the same road approaches from behind or the front — whether pedestrians on the pavement or drivers on the carriageway. On a roundabout, all traffic following the roundabout is on the same road.

Giving Way

Allowing the drivers involved to proceed without hindrance. The driver who has priority must have the idea that they have priority — they must be able to drive unhindered.

Priority Triangles (Shark's Teeth)

White triangles on the road. Usually used with, but sometimes also without, sign B-6. In both cases, these triangles mean you must give right of way to drivers on the intersecting road.

Giving Way

If priority at an intersection is not regulated by signs, road markings, or traffic lights, these are known as equivalent roads. On equivalent roads, drivers give right of way to drivers from the right.

You do not have to give a pedestrian priority — they are not classed as drivers.

There are two exceptions to this rule:

🚃
Trams always have priority. Drivers give priority to tram drivers, regardless of direction.
🛤️
Unpaved roads. Drivers on unpaved roads give right of way to drivers from both left and right on a paved road.

Priority Indicated by Signs or Road Markings

If right of way at an intersection is controlled by signs or road markings, the "right has priority" rule is then irrelevant. Various signs regulate the priority.

🟡 Being given right of way — Priority Road
B-1

Priority Road

You are on a priority road. Crossing drivers must give you priority. This sign is repeated within built-up areas before every intersection, and outside built-up areas after every intersection — indicating whether you are inside or outside a built-up area.

B-2

End of Priority Road

The B-2 sign indicates the end of the priority road. After this sign, the normal priority rules apply.

🟡 Being given right of way — Priority Intersection
B-3

Priority Intersection — Two Side Roads

You must be given priority. The thick arrow = your road. The cross marks = side roads. Side roads on both left and right. B-3, B-4 and B-5 only apply to the intersection you are approaching.

B-4

Priority Intersection — Side Road on the Left

One side road on the left. You must be given priority by the crossing drivers from that side road.

B-5

Priority Intersection — Side Road on the Right

One side road on the right. You must be given priority by the crossing drivers from that side road.

🔴 Giving right of way
B-6

Give Way

You must give priority to drivers on the intersecting road. Stopping is not mandatory — as long as you can give priority to the crossing drivers. Always combined with priority triangles (shark's teeth).

B-7

Stop — Give Way

You must give priority to drivers on the intersecting road. Stopping is always mandatory — even if you have seen there are no drivers on the intersecting road. Applied at intersections with poor visibility or high accident risk. Always combined with a stop line.

B-6 — Give Way Triangle

Stop only if needed. Always comes with priority triangles on the road surface.

B-7 — STOP Sign

Always stop — even if the road is clear. Always comes with a stop line on the road surface.

These signs and markings are also only intended for drivers. Pedestrians are not subject to these priority rules.

Examples

Scenario 1 — B-6 + Priority Triangles

When approaching sign B-6 and priority triangles, give priority to drivers on the intersecting road. Here, a horse-drawn vehicle comes from the left on the intersecting road. The lesson car must give right of way to this driver.

Scenario 2 — Pedestrian at Priority Triangles

The lesson car is approaching priority triangles. The pedestrian on the intersecting road must wait for the lesson car — the lesson car only has to give priority to drivers, not pedestrians.

Scenario 3 — B-7 Stop Sign + Pedestrian

The pedestrian must wait for the lesson car. Due to the B-7 stop sign, the lesson car must stop — but can immediately drive away if no drivers come from left or right. A pedestrian is not a driver.

Scenario 4 — Tram at Regulated Intersection

Tram drivers always have priority at an equivalent intersection. However, as soon as signs and road markings regulate the priority, the tram driver must follow these. Here, the tram driver must wait for the lesson car.
