Use of Lighting While Driving

Vehicle lighting aids visibility in the dark and — above all — helps you be seen by others. Sometimes lighting is mandatory, sometimes not. Drivers are advised to use appropriate lighting both day and night.

🔆 Dipped Headlights

🔆
Dipped Headlights

White or yellow lighting at the front of the vehicle, illuminating a good portion of the road without blinding oncoming traffic. Always permitted.

Mandatory during the day in poor visibility and at night. Applies to motor vehicle drivers, all types of mopeds, and motorised disabled vehicle drivers.

Switch on dipped headlights well before entering a tunnel.

🟡 Daytime Running Lights

🟡
Daytime Running Lights (DRL)

Available on all new passenger cars — often LED lighting next to, under, or around the headlamp. May only be used if dipped headlights are not mandatory and no other front lighting is switched on.

In most cars, DRL is on when the light switch is at OFF. When other lighting is switched on, DRL switches off automatically.

Older cars: rear lights may not illuminate together with DRL — reducing rear visibility. New cars: mandatory that taillights are lit together with DRL.

💡 Full-Beam Headlights

💡
Full-Beam

Use full-beam when dipped headlights give insufficient visibility. Full-beam shines straight ahead and can quickly blind others.

✕ Full-Beam NOT Allowed When
  • During the day
  • When encountering other road users (oncoming traffic)
  • When driving closely behind another vehicle
  • In bad weather — heavy rain, snow or fog (you will blind yourself)
💡 Blinded by Full-Beam?

If an oncoming vehicle blinds you with full-beam, flash your own full-beam to warn them. If a vehicle behind you is using full-beam, use the dimming mirror (fold the rearview mirror to the dimming position). Many modern cars have an automatically dimming mirror.

🌫️ Front Fog Lights

🌫️
Front Fog Lights

Only use when visibility is severely obstructed by fog, snow or rain. They face downwards and are not blinding, but only illuminate close to the car.

Can be used alongside dipped headlights or sidelights. In very dense fog, even dipped headlights can have a blinding effect — they are therefore not mandatory when using fog lights.

Not all passenger cars are equipped with front fog lights.

🔴 Rear Fog Light

🔴
Rear Fog Light

One or two bright red lamps at the rear — very bright and sometimes confused with brake lights. Because of this brightness, only use when visibility is less than 50 metres.

Only permitted in fog or snow. Not during rainfall — it will blind traffic behind you.

A dashboard warning light illuminates when the rear fog light is on, so you cannot accidentally leave it on.

50 m
How to judge 50 metres: Reflector posts along the road are usually 50 metres apart. If you pass one post and can only just see the next one, switch on the rear fog light.
🌧️

Rain only? Do NOT use the rear fog light during rainfall — it is only permitted in fog or snow.

🔅 Sidelights

🔅
Sidelights (Parking Lights)

White or yellow front lamps providing very little light. Located between the OFF position and dipped headlights on the light switch. Can be used in combination with front fog lights. Seldom used otherwise — usually the same as parking lights.

🔴 Rear Lights & Number Plate Lighting

🔴
Rear Lights

Normal red lamps at the back. Must be on whenever full-beam, dipped headlights, sidelights or fog lights are on at the front. Less bright than brake lights or rear fog light. On new cars, they also illuminate with daytime running lights.

🔡
Number Plate Lighting

White lighting illuminating the rear number plate. Must be lit whenever full-beam, dipped headlights, sidelights or fog lights are switched on at the front — same conditions as rear lights.

🛑 Brake Lights

🛑
Brake Lights

Three bright red lamps that activate as soon as the brake pedal is pressed. The third brake light is typically at the top or bottom centre of the rear window.

Because brake lights are very bright, unnecessary braking can cause a startle response in following traffic. Release the gas in time and maintain sufficient distance to avoid unnecessary braking.

⬜ Reverse Lights

⬜
Reverse Lights

White or yellow lights at the rear that activate when reverse gear is selected. They warn traffic behind you that the vehicle is about to drive backwards. A car may have one or two reversing lights.

✨ Special Lighting

Additional lighting may be used alongside dipped headlights or front fog lights. For example, modern cars increasingly have cornering lights — lighting that turns with the steering to illuminate the edge of a bend in the dark.

⚙️ Other / Modified Lighting

⚠ Adding or Changing Lighting

Adding extra lighting or replacing standard lighting (e.g. with xenon or LED lamps) is not always permitted and is not something you can simply do yourself. Always seek advice from someone with professional knowledge. Not all lighting sold commercially is road-legal for passenger cars.

🚛 Trailer Lighting

🚛
Trailer Lighting Requirements

A trailer must provide lighting during the day in poor visibility and at night — just like the towing vehicle. Required lighting: red rear lights, number plate lighting, and white sidelights at the front.

Front sidelights are only required on trailers wider than 1.60 metres.
