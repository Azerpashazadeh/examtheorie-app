Special Vehicles – Part 1

Some vehicles have more rights than other vehicles. These include emergency vehicles. Trams, military convoys, and funeral processions of motor vehicles also have special rights. There are also vehicles that do not have extra rights but which require extra attention — for example, larger transports.

🚑
Emergency Vehicle

A motor vehicle using both optical signals (blue flashing light) and sound signals (two-tone siren). Always give right of way to these vehicles.

🏥
Ambulance

A motor vehicle equipped and intended for the transportation of sick or injured people to and from hospital.

👨‍⚕️
Emergency Medical Services

A motor vehicle for providing emergency medical care — not necessarily transporting a patient. For example, a GP's company car used to reach a patient urgently.

🐾
Animal Ambulance

A motor vehicle for transporting sick or injured animals. Never an emergency vehicle — no blue lights or sirens, though often has extra yellow flashing lights.

Emergency Vehicles

Emergency vehicles are motor vehicles with an urgent task. They signal urgency using blue optical signals (flashing lights) and sound signals in the form of a two-tone horn (siren). Without both of these signals — or with only one — they are not emergency vehicles and do not have priority.

🔵
Both signals = Emergency Vehicle ✔

Blue flashing lights AND two-tone siren together. Full priority — all road users must give way.

❌
One signal only = No priority

Only lights, or only siren — the vehicle is not classed as an emergency vehicle and has no special priority.

ℹ Other Authorised Emergency Vehicles

Beyond police, ambulance, and fire service, other authorised services may use these signals. These include vehicles of Rijkswaterstaat (Directorate-General for Public Works and Waterways) and vehicles used for blood or organ transport.

Give Way to Emergency Vehicles

All road users must give way to emergency vehicles. There are no exceptions. However, you must do so safely — you may not cause danger, hinder others, or violate traffic rules in the process. When an emergency vehicle approaches, remain calm. The driver will make their own way as much as possible. The more predictably you act, the easier that becomes.

💡 Tips for Giving Way Safely
  • Only make room if it is safe to do so. In the meantime, continue driving at the permitted speed.
  • If a lane is free, leave it free for the emergency vehicle. Stay off the hard shoulder in case of traffic jams!
  • When on a roundabout — or approaching one with an emergency vehicle behind you — continue on the roundabout until the emergency vehicle has left it.
  • If you are in front of a red traffic light, only make room if the emergency vehicle cannot pass. Do not drive through a red light onto the intersection — waiting is often safer.
  • If an emergency vehicle wants to pass you, stay in your lane and keep right as much as possible. Never enter the verge or pavement at high speed.
  • Always make sure there are not more emergency vehicles following.
👁 Use Your Mirrors

If you don't see an emergency vehicle in time, you cannot respond properly. Check all mirrors regularly. It is not uncommon to see an emergency vehicle before you can hear it. Also maintain sufficient following distance — even at traffic lights — so you always have room to make space.

Emergency Vehicles and Traffic Rules

Emergency vehicles may ignore certain traffic rules when they have a good reason. For example, they may drive through a red light or exceed the speed limit.

🚔 Special Police Exception

Police vehicles are allowed to ignore certain traffic rules even without optical and sound signals — meaning even when they are not technically classed as an emergency vehicle at that moment.

However, this is not permitted arbitrarily — only when there is a good reason.

⚠ Important Reminder

You must never violate traffic rules yourself when giving way to an emergency vehicle. For example, do not run a red light onto a busy intersection, and never drive onto a level crossing when the lights are on — even for a fire engine.
