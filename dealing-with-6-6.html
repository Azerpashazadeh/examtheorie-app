Dealing with Accidents and Breakdowns

Anyone who participates in traffic may sooner or later be involved in a breakdown or accident. It is important to know what to do — and what not to do — in these situations.

🧰 Equipment

Although not mandatory, the following items in a passenger car are highly useful:

🔥 Fire Extinguisher

For extinguishing small or beginning fires. In the event of a large fire, exit the vehicle immediately and call the fire department.

🩹 First Aid Kit

Pressure bandages can be life-saving for serious injuries. First aid kits are of little use without knowledge — all drivers are advised to take a first aid course and regularly check the kit for expiry dates.

⚠️ Warning Triangle

When a vehicle becomes an obstacle and hazard lights are not working — or are working but insufficient — use of a warning triangle is mandatory.

🦺 Fluorescent Safety Vest

Helps passing drivers see persons waiting at the roadside. In some countries, wearing a fluorescent vest during a breakdown or accident is mandatory for all occupants.

🔨 Safety Hammer

Used to break windows if driving into water. Always strike the corner of the glass — it breaks more easily. Ideally mounted above both front doors on the ceiling for quick access after a collision.

📋 European Damage Claim Form

How to use the European accident statement

All parties fill in the front of the form — including signatures — immediately after the incident. Divide the two sheets (original + transfer sheet) and each party keeps one copy. Fill in the back at home and send to your insurance company.

🛣️ Using the Hard Shoulder

The hard shoulder should only be used in a breakdown, accident, or other emergency. Do not remain on it for longer than necessary — if possible, continue to the nearest exit, car park, or petrol station.

If you must stop, stay as far from the road as possible — if necessary, in the verge. Rijkswaterstaat (Directorate-General for Public Works and Water Management) may close a lane or arrange a tow. Contact emergency services if needed.

🚧 Accident or Breakdown on the Motorway

⚠ Safety First

Never cross the motorway, even if you are stranded in the central reservation following an accident. Wait for emergency services outside the vehicle, in the central reservation, as far from traffic as possible. Never remove vehicle parts from the carriageway yourself — leave this to the police or Rijkswaterstaat.

🔧 Rules in the Event of a Breakdown

Step-by-step breakdown procedure
  • Park the vehicle as safely as possible — in a parking space, emergency area, emergency lane, or in the verge.
  • Think about your own safety and that of your passengers. Preferably wear a safety vest. Get everyone out and take them to a safe place.
  • If necessary, place a warning triangle and/or switch on hazard lights. In darkness or poor visibility, also turn on side lights and rear lights.
  • Fix the problem if you can do so safely, or contact the emergency or breakdown service.
  • Do not sit in the vehicle while waiting. Wait at a safe distance, such as behind the crash barrier.

🚑 Rules at the Scene of an Accident

What to do at an accident
  • Always attend to your own safety first! Secure the accident scene if possible.
  • Alert the emergency services immediately.
  • Provide first aid if necessary — always have this done by the person with the most knowledge.
  • Try to avoid an overload of helpers. Keep spectators at bay and keep your distance if there is already sufficient help on site.
  • Listen to and follow the emergency responders — do not get in their way.

💧 Rules for Driving into Water

1
Turn on all lighting

Interior and exterior lights help you orient yourself and make the car visible. Leave the keys in the ignition.

2
Remove the seat belt

If the belt is jammed, cut it with the blade of the safety hammer.

3
Open or shatter the side window

The sooner you exit, the better. Electric windows often continue to work in water. If not, smash the glass with the safety hammer — always strike the corner of the window, as it breaks most easily there.

4
If windows cannot be opened or broken

Wait until the car is fully flooded inside. Only then can the door be opened. Take a big breath just before the water floods in, then push the door open and exit.

5
Swim away from the car

First swim away facing the car. When at a safe distance, turn around and swim to the side. Climb out of the water and call the emergency services.

📞 Emergency Services

In the event of any possible injury following an accident, always call 112. Adrenaline can mask pain and hide serious injuries — a person may appear unharmed but have hidden injuries.

When calling, always provide your location and the type of help needed (police, ambulance, fire department). On an autoweg or motorway, mention the street name and the nearest hectometre sign. Stay on the line until told otherwise and follow all instructions.

🛣️ Reporting Lane of an Accident

Count motorway lanes starting from the central reservation. The left lane is lane 1. On a three-lane road, the right-hand lane is lane 3.

🩺 Providing First Aid

Never move injured persons unless absolutely necessary — only if leaving them is more dangerous (e.g. railway track, fire risk). The same applies to helmets: never remove a helmet unless leaving it on poses a greater risk (e.g. risk of vomiting).

Speak to and reassure the victim; ensure they do not move. Have another person hold the victim's head if there is a risk of neck injury. If unconscious, check whether they are breathing. If not, have the most medically knowledgeable bystander resuscitate and report this to emergency services. If breathing but at risk of vomiting, place in the recovery position.

🚗 Single-Vehicle Accidents

Reporting single-vehicle accidents is mandatory when there is resulting damage to another person's property or to municipal property. Report to the property owner if possible, or to the police. Leaving the scene without providing your details is prohibited.

Collisions involving animals — cats, dogs, deer, and other wild animals — must also be reported. Call the animal police on 144 for wild animals. This is a special division within the regular police.

☢️ Hazardous Substances

In an accident involving a vehicle transporting hazardous substances, identifying what is being transported is vital for emergency services.

☢ KEMLER / GEVI Sign on Lorries

The Hazard Identification Number (GEVI / KEMLER) at the top of the orange plate indicates the danger presented by the substance. The Substance Identification Number (STID) at the bottom indicates which substance is being transported.

Examples: 1203 = petrol  |  1202 = diesel
