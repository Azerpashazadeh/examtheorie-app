Chapter 5

Special Provisions for People with Disabilities

Parking exemptions, disability parking cards — both Dutch and foreign — and the rules that give disabled road users greater flexibility on Dutch roads.

  • Exemptions for Disabled Road Users
  • Foreign Disability Parking Cards

Chapter 5 is a focused chapter with two parts. The first establishes the specific exemptions from normal traffic rules that apply to holders of a disability parking card — the well-known blue card. The second deals with disability parking cards issued outside the Netherlands and how they are recognised on Dutch roads.

Although this chapter is short, its content appears regularly in the theory exam, particularly the precise scope of what the disability parking card does and does not permit.

§ 1 — Exemptions for People with Disabilities

What holders of a valid disability parking card are permitted to do that ordinary drivers are not.

The Disability Parking Card Gehandicaptenparkeerkaart

The disability parking card — a standardised blue card with the wheelchair symbol — is issued to people with a significant and permanent mobility impairment. It must be displayed visibly on the dashboard whenever the vehicle is parked in a location where the card grants an exemption.

The card belongs to the person, not the vehicle. It may be used in any vehicle the card holder is travelling in — whether they are the driver or a passenger. If the card holder is a passenger, the driver parks on their behalf, but the card must still be displayed.

What the Card Permits

Park in disabled bays

Park in spaces reserved for disability card holders — marked with the wheelchair symbol and the sign E6. Without the card, using these spaces is prohibited.

Extended time in blue zones

In time-limited blue parking zones (blauwe zones), card holders may park for longer than the standard time limit — or in many cases without any time limit at all, depending on the local rules.

Park without paying in paid zones

In paid parking areas, card holders are often exempt from paying — subject to local authority rules. The card itself does not guarantee free parking everywhere; local signs may restrict this.

Park on yellow-line roads

On roads marked with a single yellow line (stopping restriction during specific hours), card holders may park for up to three hours outside the hours shown on the supplementary board — provided there is no supplementary board that explicitly excludes them.

Park on the carriageway of a priority road

Outside built-up areas, parking on the carriageway of a priority road (voorrangsweg) is normally prohibited for all drivers. Card holders are exempt from this prohibition.

Use pavement / footpath (snorfiets)

Riders of a specific category of snorfiets (designed for disabled persons) who hold a disability parking card may use the pavement or footpath — a permission not available to other road users.

What the Card Does NOT Permit

The disability parking card is not a universal exemption from all parking rules. It does not permit parking:

— On a pedestrian crossing or within 5 metres of one
— At a bus stop within the marked stopping area
— On a bicycle lane with a solid line
— In a tunnel
— At an intersection within 5 metres of the corner
— Alongside a double yellow line (absolute stopping prohibition)
— In locations where a no-stopping sign (C17) applies

These prohibitions apply to everyone — disability card or not.

Card Must Be Displayed Correctly Visible on dashboard

For the exemption to apply, the disability parking card must be placed visibly on the dashboard — face up, clearly readable from outside the vehicle. The card must show:

  • The wheelchair symbol facing outward
  • The expiry date clearly visible
  • The card must be valid — not expired

An expired card, a card placed face-down, or a card that is not the correct type does not grant any exemption. Enforcement officers check cards regularly.

Some municipalities use digital parking systems where the card number is registered rather than displayed physically. Always follow local instructions in these areas.
Parking Duration — Three-Hour Rule Time limit

When a disability card holder parks in a location where a time limit applies and the card grants an extended or unlimited stay, this does not mean the vehicle can be left indefinitely in all cases. In certain situations — particularly on public roads rather than in designated bays — a practical limit of three hours applies unless local rules specify otherwise.

In designated disabled parking bays (E6 sign), the duration is typically unlimited unless a supplementary board specifies a maximum time.

The Card Belongs to the Person, Not the Vehicle Personal card

This is one of the most important points about the disability parking card. It is a personal card — issued to an individual person, not to a specific vehicle or registration plate. This means:

  • The card can be used in any vehicle — the card holder's own car, a taxi, a friend's car, a rental vehicle
  • The card holder must be present in the vehicle when it is parked using the card's exemption
  • Using the card without the card holder present — for example, a family member parking without the disabled person — is misuse and a violation
Common Exam Question: Driver or Passenger?

The theory exam frequently tests whether candidates understand that the card holder does not have to be the driver. If a disabled person is a passenger and the driver parks using the disability card to drop them off or accompany them, this is permitted — as long as the card holder is in the vehicle when it is parked. The card grants the exemption based on the person's presence, not their role in the vehicle.

§ 2 — Disability Parking Cards Issued Outside the Netherlands

How foreign disability parking cards are treated on Dutch roads — and under what conditions they are recognised.

EU Cards Are Recognised European Union

A disability parking card issued by any other EU member state is recognised in the Netherlands. The EU has standardised the disability parking card across member states — same blue colour, same wheelchair symbol, same format — specifically to ensure mutual recognition. A Dutch enforcement officer must accept a valid EU-issued card from any member state.

The same conditions apply as for Dutch cards: the card must be valid (not expired), displayed correctly on the dashboard, and the card holder must be present in the vehicle.

Non-EU Cards — Recognised by Treaty Treaty countries

Cards issued by non-EU countries are also recognised in the Netherlands — but only if that country has signed a relevant international agreement or treaty that the Netherlands is party to. These treaties typically require the card to meet certain minimum standards.

Countries that have signed the relevant UN or European agreements on road traffic — even outside the EU — generally have their disability parking cards recognised in the Netherlands. In practice, this covers most European countries including Norway, Switzerland, Iceland and others.

Recognised foreign cards
  • All EU member states (standardised format)
  • Norway, Iceland, Liechtenstein (EEA)
  • Switzerland
  • Other countries that have signed the relevant traffic treaties with the Netherlands
Same conditions apply
  • Card must be valid — not expired
  • Displayed correctly on dashboard, face up
  • Card holder must be present in the vehicle
  • The same parking rules and exemptions apply as for Dutch card holders
What to Do If Uncertain Practical advice

If you are travelling to the Netherlands with a foreign disability parking card and are uncertain whether it is recognised, check in advance with the issuing authority in your country or with the RDW (Dutch Vehicle Authority). The general rule is: if your country is in the EU or has a relevant treaty with the Netherlands, your card will be accepted. When in doubt, carry documentation alongside the card.

Same Exemptions — Same Limits

A foreign disability parking card recognised in the Netherlands grants exactly the same exemptions as a Dutch card — no more, no less. Foreign card holders may use designated disabled bays, park in blue zones with extended time, and benefit from the other exemptions described in §1. They are also subject to the same limitations — they cannot park at bus stops, on crossings or alongside double yellow lines.

Misuse of a Foreign Card Prohibited

Misuse of a foreign disability parking card — for example, using it without the card holder present, using an expired card, or using a card that belongs to someone else — is a violation in the Netherlands just as it would be with a Dutch card. Dutch enforcement officers can and do check foreign cards. Misuse may result in a fine and, in serious cases, referral to the issuing authority.

Chapter 5 — What to Take Away

The disability parking card is a personal card — it follows the person, not the vehicle. It permits parking in disabled bays, extended stays in blue zones, parking on priority road carriageways outside built-up areas, and parking on single-yellow-line roads for up to three hours. It does not permit parking at crossings, bus stops, on double yellow lines or in tunnels.

Foreign cards from EU countries are automatically recognised. Cards from non-EU countries are recognised if a treaty is in place. The same conditions and exemptions apply to foreign cards as to Dutch ones. For the exam: remember that the card holder must be present in the vehicle, that the card must be displayed correctly, and that the absolute stopping prohibitions apply to everyone regardless of disability status.
