General Requirements – Part 4

Lighting Requirements

To maintain adequate visibility both inside a car and on the road, passenger cars must be equipped with specific lighting.

Rear light
Red
Seen from the rear, indicates the vehicle's presence and width.
Reverse light
White or Yellow
Illuminates the road behind and warns other road users the vehicle is reversing.
Dipped headlights
White or Yellow
Illuminates the road ahead without blinding or obstructing other road users.
Full-beam headlights
White or Yellow
Illuminates the road in front over a long distance.
Rear fog light
Red
Makes the vehicle more visible at the rear in dense fog.
Brake light
Red
Indicates to road users behind that the vehicle is intentionally decelerating.
Indicator
Amber
Informs other road users that the driver intends to change direction left or right.
Sidelights
White or Yellow
Seen from the front, indicates the presence and width of the vehicle.
Hazard warning light
Same as indicators
All indicators operating simultaneously to signal a temporary danger to other road users.
Rear number plate light
White
Makes the rear number plate readable in the dark. May not beam out of the rear.
Retro-reflector rear
Red
Does not emit light — reflects light beams from other vehicles. Makes stationary vehicles visible without their own lighting.

Please note that the positioning of lights may vary per passenger car.

Lighting Colours

Most vehicles are equipped with red, amber and white lighting. Where red is generally on the rear, white on the front and amber on the sides of the vehicle. Some lighting deviates from this — for example, some lights on the front may also be yellow instead of white and some lights on the rear may also be amber instead of red.

💡

Mnemonic: White light comes towards you, red light goes away from you. That is why the reverse light is also white, despite being on the rear of the vehicle.

Side Indicators

A car's indicators must be clearly visible from all directions. On most modern passenger cars, this lighting wraps around the car's corners, which means the turn signal is also visible from the side. If this is not the case, additional indicators must be equipped on the side of the vehicle.

Permitted Lighting

Cornering lighting
White
Supplementary light that assists when turning corners.
Daytime running light
White
Increases visibility of the vehicle during the day.
Front fog light
White or Yellow
Illuminates the road ahead in fog or poor visibility conditions.
Parking light
Front white, rear red
Indicates the presence of a parked vehicle. Usually the same as the sidelight.

Additional Marker Lighting and Reflectors

Vehicles that are larger than average must be clearly marked with lighting and additional reflectors to alert other drivers.

  • Vehicles wider than 2.10 metres must have two white marker lights at the front and two red marker lights at the rear.
  • Vehicles longer than 6 metres must be fitted with amber-coloured side marker lights and amber-coloured retro-reflectors on the side. The rear of these may be red instead of amber.
↔ Width > 2.10 m → marker lights required ↕ Length > 6 m → side marker lights & reflectors required

Marker lighting

Lighting on the outermost corners of a vehicle that indicates the size of the vehicle (end-outline marker lights). Passenger cars may also be equipped with additional marker lights and retro-reflectors.

Miscellaneous Lighting Requirements

In addition to presence and mandatory colour, passenger car lighting must meet the following requirements:

  • The mandatory lights must work well and the mandatory retro-reflectors must reflect well.
  • The light luminaires (light holders) must be undamaged and properly attached to the vehicle.
  • The glass of the luminaires must not be broken or removed, and they must allow enough light to shine through properly.
  • Lights and retro-reflectors that have the same function must be placed symmetrically, and the lights must have the same brightness and size.
  • The translucent parts of the mandatory lights may be shielded for a maximum of 25%.
  • The level control of the dipped headlights must work properly — this is the manual height adjustment used when the vehicle leans backwards due to heavier loading, to avoid blinding oncoming traffic.

Dashboard Warning Lights

Some lights should never be switched on accidentally. Warning lights for this purpose are present on the dashboard:

In addition to an indicator warning light, indicators may also be accompanied by a sound signal when switched on.

Prohibited Lighting and Retro-reflectors

Passenger cars may not feature any lighting or retro-reflectors other than those previously described. Present lighting:

  • May not be blinding — except for the full-beam headlights.
  • May not be blinking — except for the indicators, hazard warning lights and the emergency brake signal (brake lights automatically blink during an emergency stop).

When an indicator is switched on, the lights on that side of the vehicle may blink. All other lighting — such as the blue lights featured on emergency vehicles — is prohibited for passenger cars, regardless of whether it is on or off. Your vehicle may not at any time or in any way resemble an emergency vehicle.

Interior lighting in passenger cars must not beam light outside the vehicle.

Securing Trailers

If a passenger car is equipped with a tow bar, this must be properly secured and must not be damaged, broken, or corroded.

Mandatory and Permitted Sound Signals

Passenger cars must be equipped with a properly functioning horn, with a fixed pitch. Car horns playing a melody are not permitted.

Passenger cars may also be equipped with:

  • A beeping reverse signal.
  • A sound signal on electric passenger cars that sounds at a speed of up to 25 km/h.
  • An alarm system with sound signal.

All other sound-generating devices are prohibited on passenger cars.
