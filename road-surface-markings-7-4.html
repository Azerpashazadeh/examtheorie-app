Road Surface Markings

In addition to traffic signs and traffic lights, traffic is also controlled by markings on the road surface — lines, painted numbers, priority triangles, and arrows.

Edge Line

Line on the edge of the carriageway.

Centre / Axis Line

Line in the middle, separating your lane from oncoming traffic.

Lane Line

Line dividing one direction of the carriageway into separate lanes.

━ Solid Lines

Solid lines may not be crossed. A solid axis line means you may not drive on the left (oncoming traffic side). A solid lane line means you may not change lanes or overtake.

✓ Exceptions — when you may cross a solid line
  • An edge line may be crossed, e.g. to park in the verge.
  • A solid line between the carriageway and an adjacent refuge, hard shoulder, or rush-hour lane may be crossed if you need to use it.
  • If there is a broken line on your side of the solid line, you may cross the solid line.
  • Drivers allowed on a cycle lane with a solid line may cross it, e.g. to overtake a cyclist.

╌ Broken Lines and Warning Lines

A broken axis or lane line may be crossed — you may overtake or change lanes if safe to do so.

A warning line is a broken line where the dashes are longer than the gaps. Overtaking is still permitted, but is more dangerous here than with a normal broken line.

⣿ Hatched Markings and Dividing Points

Prohibited
Hatched Road Markings

Oblique stripe areas — may not be driven on. Used where a lane ends and drivers must merge.

Prohibited
Dividing Points

Found at acceleration and deceleration lanes on autowegen/motorways. May not be crossed.

⚠ Rush-hour lane exception

Hatched markings and dividing points no longer apply when they are on an open rush-hour lane. In that case, driving over them is permitted.

↙️ Pre-sorting Lanes, Deceleration Lanes, and Arrows

Pre-sorting lanes divide the carriageway into lanes for different directions at large intersections. Arrows indicate the direction. At the intersection, you must follow the arrow on your lane. Pre-sorting left and then going straight is not permitted.

A hammerhead arrow on a pre-sorting lane indicates a side road ahead — you may not turn into that side road.

Once there are arrows on a deceleration lane, you may no longer return to the main carriageway. This prevents using the deceleration lane to pass traffic jams.

⛔ Stop Line and Priority Triangles

At a STOP sign, a stop line must be on the road surface. You must stop before it — regardless of whether other traffic is present. The line at a traffic light is also a stop line, but you only stop there if the light is yellow or red.

Priority triangles (shark teeth) — white triangles on the road surface — mean: give priority to drivers on the intersecting road. Usually combined with sign B-6, but they have the same meaning even without it.

🚌 Bus Lanes and Bus Carriageways

BUS
BUS lane

Only bus drivers (including coaches) and public buses may use this lane. Emergency vehicles on an urgent task may also use it.

LIJNBUS
LIJNBUS lane

Only public bus drivers may use this lane. Emergency vehicles on an urgent task may also use it.

🚲 Cycle Lanes and Suggestion Lanes

An official cycle lane must have a bicycle symbol on the road surface and be separated by a broken or solid line. Without the bicycle symbol, it is a suggestion lane — no legal status, anyone may use it, and parking on it is permitted.

A bike box is an extended cycle lane in front of a pre-sorting lane at traffic lights, designed to keep cyclists more visible and safe.

🟡 Yellow Lines on the Side of the Road

╌ Broken Yellow Kerb Line

Parking prohibited next to this line. Stopping to pick up or drop off passengers is permitted.

━ Solid Yellow Kerb Line

Parking AND standing still are both prohibited — including picking up or dropping off passengers.

🟡🔵 Yellow Lines on Road Surface & Blue Lines

Yellow lines on the road surface are temporary, during roadworks. They override the normal white markings. Speed limits are often lower and lanes can be narrower.

Blue lines in parking bays indicate that a parking disc is mandatory.

🔲 Other Markings

No stopping
Blocked/Checkered (Bus Stops)

Found at bus stops on the pavement. No stopping allowed, except to pick up or drop off passengers.

Carriageway
Block Marking

Between lanes at acceleration, deceleration, weaving, and pre-sorting lanes. Exceeding it allowed until it transitions into a solid line.

Warning
Zigzag Lines

Warn of a dangerous point such as a crossing or intersection. No priority indication — reduce speed wisely.

Aid
Auxiliary Markings

Road-painted aids: speed limits on the surface, zone entry/exit lines, speed bump markings.

📐 Speed Limit Road Markings

On newer roads, the road markings (lines) suggest the speed limit, but are not legally binding. Always rely on official signs for the actual limit.

100
km/h — Autoweg

Solid edge lines + two solid or broken white axis lines with a green line between them. The green line identifies an autoweg. If the white axis lines are solid, overtaking is prohibited. If broken, overtaking is allowed.

80
km/h — Road outside built-up area

Broken edge lines + one or two solid or broken white axis lines — no green line. Solid axis = no overtaking; broken axis = overtaking allowed.

60
km/h — Zone outside built-up area

Broken edge lines placed further from the verge. Sometimes has red suggestion cycle lanes. No axis line. Sometimes no lines at all. A 60 km/h zone sign must still be present!

📌 Not legally binding

Speed limit road markings are not legally binding — they only suggest the speed limit. The actual limit is always indicated by official signs.
