Safe Transport

Certain rules exist regarding the use of seats, seat belts, and child restraint systems to ensure that drivers, passengers, and especially young passengers travel as safely as possible.

💺

Seat: A construction that can accommodate an adult. This can be an individual seat or part of a row.

💺 Seats

Both the driver and all passengers must sit on a designated seat while in traffic. Being in the boot or cargo area while the vehicle is moving is prohibited. Sitting or standing in a trailer or caravan while it is being towed is also prohibited.

⚠ Exceptions to the Seating Rules
  • Transport in a public bus
  • Passengers in a wheelchair, if it can be secured properly
  • Children under 3 years during transportation in a bus
  • Transporting patients in ambulances or other berths
  • Passenger transport during parades at events
  • Children older than 8 years on the rear rack of a bicycle — not allowed on a snorfiets!
🚲 Young Children on Bikes

Passengers under the age of 8 may only be transported on bicycles, snorfietsen, and mopeds if seated in an appropriate seat with sufficient support for the back, hands, and feet.

🔒 Seat Belts

Seat belt use is mandatory for all drivers and passengers of: passenger cars, company cars, three-wheeled motor vehicles with closed bodywork, and brommobielen.

Never transport more passengers than there are seat belts. Today's passenger cars must have seat belts fitted on all seats — usually three-point belts across the hip and chest.

✅ Seat Belt — Key Rules

Seat belts are always mandatory for all drivers and passengers of passenger cars. The three-point belt must run well across your hips and chest. Do not run the top part behind your back or under your armpit.

Seat belts are also compulsory for pregnant women. The hip part must be worn well under the abdomen and the shoulder part well above the abdomen. Running the belt over the abdomen poses an additional risk to unborn children.

Under 12
Driver's Responsibility

The driver is responsible for ensuring children under 12 wear their seat belt.

12 & older
Passenger's Own Responsibility

Passengers aged 12 and older are themselves responsible for wearing a seat belt.

🛡️ Wearing a Seat Belt Correctly

Even at speeds as low as 30 km/h, an unbelted occupant may sustain fatal injuries. Airbags are of little use without a seat belt and may even cause additional injury.

✅
Wear it as intended

Not as a hip belt only, and not with the oblique part under your armpit. The hip part should be tight over the hip; the oblique part halfway between neck and shoulder. Many belts are adjustable in height at the top.

🔧
Pull tightly after clicking in

A belt that is too loose does not tighten properly — or tightens too late — in a collision.

🧥
Remove thick sweaters and winter jackets

The thicker the clothing, the less the belt fits the body. This extra buffer means the belt tightens later in a collision, bringing the body further forward.

💧
Always wear a belt near water

Some say it is harder to exit a submerged vehicle with a belt on — but impact with water without a belt can cause serious injury or unconsciousness, making escape impossible. Always wear the belt; undo it before exiting.

🪑 Using the Headrest

A properly adjusted headrest can prevent head and neck injuries in a rear-end collision. The top of the headrest should align with the top of the head. If it cannot go high enough, set it as high as possible. Some headrests can also be tilted forwards to reduce the gap between the head and the headrest.

🏗️

Body: The steel construction that gives the passenger car its strength — the "skeleton" of the car.

👶 Child Restraint Systems

Children under 18 years of age and less than 1.35 metres must use an approved child restraint system adapted to the child's size and weight. There are four groups:

Group 0+ — Baby Car Seat (0–13 kg)

For babies and small children up to about 18 months. Mounted against the direction of travel. Used until the child weighs ~13 kg or sits with their head above the seat shell. Secured via seat belt or Isofix, equipped with its own baby belt.

Group I — Child Car Seat (9–18 kg)

Usually facing the direction of travel. Secured with seat belt or Isofix. Suitable from about one year old. The child is secured by the seat's own belt or a "restraint" block secured with the three-point belt.

Group II & III — Booster Seat (15–25 kg / 22–36 kg)

Usually combined into one chair with a removable backrest. Use the version with backrest for as long as possible — it offers the best protection. The child is secured with the standard three-point belt, which the booster positions correctly over the body.

💥 Disabling Airbags

⚠️
MANDATORY: Disable the Front Airbag

When using a child seat placed against the direction of travel in the front, you must deactivate the passenger airbag. An activating airbag will launch the rear-facing seat — and child — through the car. This is a mandatory legal requirement.

When the airbag is active, the car seat must face the direction of travel, and the front seat should be pushed as far back as possible.

✅ Using Child Restraint Systems Correctly

Ensure the seat is properly secured

Isofix seats click directly to the car body and are most stable. Belt-fastened seats must be correctly threaded — read the instructions carefully.

Disable the passenger airbag (rear-facing)

Mandatory when transporting a child against the direction of travel on the front seat. A deploying airbag can be fatal.

Remove thick sweaters and coats

A child in a winter coat is not well protected. The belts may not close tightly enough and the child could be launched from the seat in a collision.

Stay in the same group as long as possible

Every higher group offers less protection. Do not switch to a larger group earlier than necessary.

Be cautious with second-hand seats

Always check whether a used seat has been in an accident. Like belts and helmets, child restraints that have already protected in a crash may no longer function properly. If in doubt, do not use it.

🚌 Child Restraint System Exceptions

When a Child Restraint System Is Not Mandatory
  • In taxis and public buses — not mandatory, but children must not be in the front row of seats
  • If a third child seat no longer fits due to two other child seats in the back, the third child (preferably the oldest, and in any case older than 3 years) may use only a seat belt in the back
  • For incidental transport (e.g. a children's party), children older than 3 years but less than 1.35 m can be transported in the back with only a seat belt
⚠️

Warning! Being legally permitted does not mean it is the safest choice. If it is possible to use a child restraint system for a child under 1.35 m, this is always preferable.

⛑️ Helmet Requirement

For mopeds, motorcycles, brommobielen without a closed body, motorised tricycles, and quads — instead of a seat belt, a helmet requirement applies. You are obliged to wear an approved helmet on these vehicles.

Under 12
Driver's Responsibility

The driver is responsible for ensuring children under 12 wear a helmet.

12 & older
Own Responsibility

Passengers aged 12 and older are themselves responsible for wearing a helmet.
