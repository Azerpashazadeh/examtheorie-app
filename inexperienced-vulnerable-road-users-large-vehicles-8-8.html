Inexperienced & Vulnerable Road Users, Large Vehicles

As a car driver you regularly encounter inexperienced and vulnerable road users. You have a responsibility to keep it safe — even when they do not follow the rules. You must also account for large vehicles and their limitations.

👶 Children

J-21 Children

Children have less overview than adults, limited experience, and their brains are not yet fully developed to assess all risks. They play on pavements, hide between parked cars, and cross roads suddenly chasing a ball.

In residential areas: reduce speed. Look between and under parked cars for children's feet. If children are on the pavement, always assume they may step onto the road.

🚶 Pedestrians

J-23 Pedestrians

Pedestrians are very difficult to see in the dark. On roads without a pavement or footpath, do not drive fully to the right. Pedestrians are not always consciously engaged in traffic. Recognise pedestrian crossings in time.

🚲 Cyclists, Moped Riders and Snorfietsers

J-24 Cyclists & moped riders

When turning right, check for cyclists and snorfietsers who may overtake you on the right and be in your blind spot. They are balancing vehicles that can sway unexpectedly. Maintain a lateral distance of about 1.5 metres. Be alert at cycle path crossings and in darkness.

🛵 Moped riders on the carriageway

In built-up areas, moped riders often use the carriageway. Keep sufficient distance and only overtake if you can do so safely. Pay attention to signs indicating that moped riders must use the carriageway.

Moped riders should pass the sign on the indicated side. Be aware of which side applies.

🐴 Horse Riders and Horse Transport

🐴 Horses — flight animals

Horses startle naturally — this can be dangerous for rider, handler, or a horse trailer on the road. Approach calmly: reduce speed and keep your distance. Only pass if you can do so quietly and with plenty of space. Never use the horn near a horse. Keep safe distance from horse trailers.

🚗 Brommobiel Drivers

45 km/h max

Brommobielen can be driven from age 16 — many drivers are inexperienced. Maximum speed is 45 km/h, even on roads where others drive much faster. The speed difference can be dangerous. Recognised by a round sticker/sign with red border and black "45" on the rear.

🚜 Vehicles with Limited Speed

≤25 km/h

Agricultural vehicles, road sweepers, and municipal lawn mowers are limited-speed vehicles. Those with a maximum speed of up to 25 km/h are recognised by a truncated triangle on the rear.

Agricultural vehicles are often very large — overtaking is more difficult. The driver may have limited visibility due to coupled machines or trailers. Be especially careful when a slow vehicle turns left or right while you are overtaking.

🚛 Lorry Drivers

Lorries have multiple, considerable blind spots — in front, beside, and behind. If you cannot see the driver (directly or in a mirror), the driver cannot see you. Stay out of blind spots as much as possible. Large vehicles also need more space when cornering.

The red areas are blind spots where the driver has poor or no overview. Avoid staying in these areas as much as possible.

LZV (Long Heavy Transport) — up to 25.25 m instead of the normal 18 m. Mainly used on motorways for longer journeys. Recognised by the warning sign: "Caution! Extra long!"

👴 The Elderly and the Disabled

Older people may have reduced vision, poorer mobility, slower reactions, and reduced hearing. Give them more time when crossing. Older drivers may seem doubtful — do not respond with frustration; give them space.

Blind person crossing

Blind persons use a white stick with red rings and/or a guide dog. Persons on crutches, walkers, or with a walking frame are always allowed to cross first. Blind persons indicate they want to cross by sticking their stick forward.

"SH" plate on the rear of a vehicle means SlechtHorend (hearing impaired). Deaf and hard-of-hearing persons cannot hear you coming — they may be startled or unaware of your presence.
