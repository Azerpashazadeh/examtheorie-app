RDW · 185 DAYS · Updated for 2026 CBR & RVV Rules

Exchanging a Foreign Driving Licence in the Netherlands (RDW) vs Taking the CBR Exam

Whether you can drive in the Netherlands with your foreign licence or exchange it at the municipality (RDW) depends on where it was issued and whether you hold the 30% tax ruling. Here are the exact rules so you never get caught driving uninsured after the 185-day deadline.

1. The RDW 185-Day Rule for Non-EU Driving Licences

If you move to the Netherlands and register at a Dutch municipality (BRP) holding a valid driving licence issued outside the EU/EFTA (for example from Turkey, the US, India, Syria, Morocco, Brazil, South Africa, etc.), you may drive in the Netherlands with that licence for a maximum of 185 days (approx. 6 months) from your municipal registration date.

Critical Warning: Once those 185 days expire, driving with an unexchanged non-EU licence is illegal and leaves you uninsured. Also note that while RDW processes an exchange application, your original licence is sent to RDW and you may not drive until you pick up your Dutch card.

Frequently Asked Questions

How long can I drive in the Netherlands with a non-EU driving licence?

You can drive for up to 185 days from the date you first register as a resident in a Dutch municipality (BRP), provided your foreign licence remains valid.

Can I exchange my non-EU driving licence if I have the 30% ruling?

Yes. With a valid Belastingdienst 30% ruling decision letter (Beschikking bewijsregel) and a CBR Gezondheidsverklaring, RDW allows you to exchange a valid driving licence from any country without taking a CBR exam.

What happens if my country is not on the RDW list and I do not have the 30% ruling?

You cannot exchange your licence directly; you must pass the standard CBR car theory exam (50 questions) and the CBR practical driving test through a Dutch driving school.
