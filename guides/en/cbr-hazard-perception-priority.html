CBR Hazard Perception & Dutch Priority Rules (Voorrang) Explained

Priority questions (voorrang) and hazard perception account for roughly half of the mistakes on the CBR car theory exam. Follow this exact 4-step Dutch legal hierarchy to solve every intersection question accurately.

1. The 4-Level Dutch Traffic Hierarchy

Whenever multiple rules seem to conflict at an intersection, Dutch law applies this strict top-to-bottom order:

  1. Instructions from authorized traffic controllers (police, verkeersregelaars) — override everything, even red traffic lights.
  2. Working traffic lights — override road signs and shark teeth placed at the same junction.
  3. Traffic signs & road markings (e.g. B1, B6, STOP, haaientanden) — override default equal-junction rules.
  4. General traffic rules (RVV 1990) — apply when there are no controllers, working lights, or priority signs.

2. Equal Intersections (Gelijkwaardige Kruispunten) & Trams

At a paved intersection with no priority signs, lights, or shark teeth:

  • Drivers from the right have priority (rechts gaat voor) — remember that cyclists, moped riders, and horse riders ARE drivers (bestuurders) under Dutch law! Pedestrians are not drivers, so at an equal intersection a pedestrian crossing a side road from your right does not get priority under right-before-left.
  • The Tram Exception: At an equal intersection, a tram ALWAYS has priority — whether it approaches from the left or the right, and even if the tram is turning! A tram only yields when facing a red light, B6 sign, STOP sign, or haaientanden.
  • Unpaved Roads: Drivers emerging from an unpaved dirt/forest track (onverharde weg) must yield to all drivers on a paved road.

3. Turning Rules: "Rechtdoor op dezelfde weg gaat voor"

Golden Turning Rule (RVV Art. 18): Drivers who turn left or right must give way to ALL road users continuing straight on the same road — including pedestrians and cyclists!
  • If a cyclist rides straight in the cycle lane beside you while you turn right, the cyclist goes first (always check your right mirror and right shoulder blind spot — dode hoek).
  • If a pedestrian walks straight along the sidewalk of the main road you are currently driving on and crosses the side street you are turning into, the pedestrian goes first because you are both on the same road.
  • Short turn before long turn (Korte bocht gaat voor lange bocht): If you turn right and an oncoming vehicle turns left into the same street, you go first.

4. Exits (Uitrit) & Special Manoeuvres (Bijzondere Verrichtingen — Art. 54)

When performing a special manoeuvre — leaving an uitrit (driveway / continuous raised sidewalk across an exit), pulling away from the curb, reversing, doing a U-turn, or changing lanes — you must yield to ALL other road users, including pedestrians.

5. Winning Strategy for Animated CBR Hazard Perception

  • Immediate Braking Triggers: Children or balls near parked vans, obscured intersections with less than clear stopping sight, cyclists swerving around obstacles/open car doors, or brake lights ahead on wet/icy asphalt.
  • Releasing the Accelerator (Anticipating): Approaching a blind bend, busy bus stop where passengers might cross, amber traffic lights, or narrowing residential streets.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does a tram always have priority in the Netherlands?

At an equal intersection without signs or markings, yes — a tram always has priority from left or right, even when turning. However, if the tram faces shark teeth (haaientanden), a B6/STOP sign, or a red light, the tram must yield.

Who must you yield to when leaving an uitrit (exit construction)?

Under RVV Article 54, when leaving an uitrit (recognizable by a continuous sidewalk and sloped curbstones), you must yield to ALL road users — including pedestrians walking on the sidewalk.

Do cyclists from the right have priority at an unmarked Dutch intersection?

Yes! Under Dutch law, cyclists are classified as drivers (bestuurders), so at an equal intersection cyclists approaching from your right have priority.
