Traffic Insight and Driving Behaviour

Traffic awareness and proper vehicle control are crucial to participating safely in busy traffic. How you interact with other drivers is just as important as knowing the rules. Some situations cannot be resolved with rules alone — a good driver puts others' interests first where necessary.

😡
Antisocial Driving

Putting your own interests first. Taking priority out of turn, speeding, ignoring traffic lights, cutting off others, reacting aggressively when confronted.

🤝
Social & Defensive Driving

Predicting and helping correct others' mistakes. Looking ahead, adjusting speed in time, accounting for unexpected situations, letting struggling drivers merge.

🎯
Decisive Driving

Making the right decisions on time and implementing them quickly. Being clear about intentions. Requires both knowledge of rules and traffic awareness.

😡 Antisocial and Aggressive Driving

Road users that put their own interests first behave antisocially, putting themselves and others at risk. Examples include taking priority out of turn, speeding, ignoring traffic lights, cutting off others, and reacting aggressively when challenged.

Opening the car door without looking properly is antisocial behaviour.
Texting while driving is antisocial and downright dangerous.
⚖️ Article 5 of the Road Traffic Act — "Kapstokartikel"

Behaving in such a way that causes hinder or danger to other road users, or that could lead to this, is prohibited.

Police can invoke this article. Violations may result in losing your licence, heavy fines, an obligatory Educational Behaviour and Traffic Measure (EMG), or even appearing before a judge.

🤝 Social and Defensive Driving

A good driver puts their own interests aside when necessary. Beyond following rules and avoiding errors, this involves predicting and recognising the mistakes of others and helping to correct them. Giving way to drivers struggling to merge is also social driving behaviour.

Social driving requires defensive driving — looking ahead, adjusting speed in time, and accounting for unexpected situations.

Overtaking a horse rider calmly and with space is social and safe driving behaviour.
Making room for motorcyclists filtering through traffic jams is social driving behaviour.
🔭 Anticipation

Looking ahead and thinking ahead. Being prepared to react at a moment's notice. Recognising potentially dangerous situations in time and reacting accordingly — that reaction is called defensive driving.

Taking into account children playing nearby and reacting immediately if a ball rolls onto the road — that is anticipation.
⚠️ Important clarification

Neither anticipation nor defensive driving means driving extra slowly, or giving way when you have right of way. It means empathising with other road users, understanding their intentions and what they expect from you, and adjusting your behaviour accordingly.

🎯 Decisive Driving

Decisive driving means making the right decisions on time and implementing them quickly. It also means being clear about your intentions — which requires knowledge of traffic rules and traffic awareness.

The more experienced the driver, the easier decisive driving becomes. The terms defensive driving, decisive driving, and anticipation are inextricably linked. Looking and thinking as far ahead as possible remains the most important element.
