Communication in Traffic

Communication with other road users is essential — to signal your intentions or to warn of imminent danger. Depending on the situation, you use light signals, sound, or gestures. There must always be a good reason for using them.

Anti-social hand gestures, unnecessary full-beams, excessive horn use or unnecessary indicator use are not permitted and may be penalised. Unnecessary noise — such as a broken exhaust, revving while stationary, or extremely loud music — is also prohibited.

🤚 Hand Gestures

In some situations a friendly gesture is the clearest way to communicate. For example, indicating to a hesitant pedestrian that it is safe to cross. Drivers of larger vehicles may gesture to signal they are giving way to you.

✅ Appropriate Use
  • Signal to a pedestrian that it is safe to cross
  • Indicate you are waiving your right of way
  • Signal to a larger vehicle that they may enter
✕ Never Do This
  • Aggressive gestures (e.g. middle finger)
  • Frustrated gestures at other drivers
  • Any gesture that could provoke aggression
💡 Tip

Count to ten and resolve any situations as safely and calmly as possible. Avoiding aggression prevents dangerous situations in traffic.

📯 Horn

📯
Horn Rules

Every passenger car must be equipped with a horn. Driving with a broken horn is prohibited.

Only use the horn to avert imminent danger — for example, if you are almost hit because someone has not seen you.

Do not sound the horn to greet a friend, as a farewell when driving away, or out of anger or frustration.

⚠️ Hazard Lights

⚠️
Hazard Lights Rules

Hazard lights must work properly at all times. You may only use them to avert imminent danger.

If you suffer a breakdown, using hazard lights is mandatory — unless you are using a warning triangle instead.

When approaching a traffic jam, you may switch on hazard lights to warn following traffic.

✅ Permitted Uses
  • Breakdown — mandatory unless using warning triangle
  • Approaching a traffic jam — warn following traffic
  • Imminent danger situation
✕ Not Permitted
  • During loading or unloading
  • As a substitute for proper parking behaviour
  • When there is no danger

🔦 Indicators

🔦
When Indicators Are Mandatory

Indicator use is mandatory before and during: turning, pulling out (driving away), overtaking, getting in lane, exiting, changing lanes, and important lateral movements.

Indicating too early can cause confusion. Not indicating at all is equally dangerous.

Misuse or non-use of indicators is a criminal offence. Indicators must always work.

💡 Flashing Signal with Full-Beam Headlights

💡
Full-Beam Flash Rules

You may only flash full-beam to avert imminent danger.

Permitted examples: warning an oncoming driver who has their full-beam on and is blinding you, or warning a driver who is using no lighting at all in the dark.

✅ Permitted
  • Warn oncoming driver: full-beam blinding you
  • Warn driver with no lights in the dark
  • Avert any imminent danger
✕ Not Permitted
  • Warn oncoming traffic of a police check
  • Signal that you want to overtake / pass
  • As a general greeting or communication

🛑 Using the Brake Lights

The brake lights activate as soon as you press the brake pedal. Unnecessary braking wastes energy and is annoying for traffic behind you.

🛑
Brake Light as a Warning

You can use the brake pedal as a warning by tapping it briefly and lightly before actually braking — for example before turning or braking for a pedestrian crossing. This gives following traffic time to increase their distance.

Never use brake lights deliberately to startle a tailgater. This is dangerous, encourages aggression, and if it causes an accident it is a crime.

If there is a tailgater behind you, increase your own following distance so you do not have to brake abruptly.

🔺 Warning Triangle

It is not mandatory to carry a warning triangle in a passenger car. However, placing one is sometimes obligatory — for example when you break down or have an accident and pose a danger to approaching traffic.

🔺 Warning Triangle — Key Rules
  • Placing is not required if hazard lights are on and clearly visible to other traffic
  • If the vehicle is just after a bend or over a hill and cannot be seen in time, a warning triangle must be placed
  • Place the triangle ~30 metres from the vehicle, in the direction of approaching traffic
  • The triangle may be used in conjunction with hazard lights
  • Only compulsory for motor vehicles with more than two wheels and for trailers
⚠ Placement Direction

Place the triangle on the side from which traffic is approaching your vehicle. If you are on the right side of the road forming an obstacle for traffic behind you, place it 30 metres behind your car.
