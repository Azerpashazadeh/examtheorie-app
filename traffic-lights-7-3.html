Traffic Lights

In addition to standard tricolour traffic lights, there are tram and bus lights, level crossing and bridge lights, lane lights, pedestrian lights, and more.

🔴🟡🟢 Tricolour Traffic Lights

The most common traffic lights. Found at larger intersections with red, yellow ("orange"), and green. Lamps can be circular, directional arrows, or symbols such as a bicycle (also covering mopeds, snorfietsen, speed pedelecs, and disabled vehicles).

🔴 Red

Stop. Driving is prohibited.

🟡 Yellow

Stop — unless you are so close to the light that you cannot stop in a reasonable way. In that case you may drive on.

🟢 Green

Keep driving. Not driving on at a green light is prohibited.

💛 Flashing Yellow

Traffic light is not working. Approach carefully. Traffic signs and road markings governing priority apply again.

↩️ Turning Right at Red Lights

In some cases a supplementary sign is placed near the traffic light permitting a right turn on red:

Rechtsaf voor fietsers vrij

Cyclists and snorfietsers may turn right — even at red.

Rechtsaf voor (brom)fietsers vrij

Cyclists, snorfietsers, moped riders, and speed pedelec riders may turn right at red. Brommobielen may not!

⚠ Right turn at red — give way!

When turning right on a red light, traffic that has a green light still has right of way. Always give way.

🔵 Round Lamp vs Arrow Lamp

⭕ Round lamp

Oncoming or parallel traffic may have green at the same time. Two rules apply:

1. Through traffic has right of way over turning traffic.
2. Right-turning traffic has right of way over left-turning traffic.

➡️ Arrow-shaped lamp

Turning left or right with an arrow light means you will not encounter conflicting traffic. You can turn unhindered — no oncoming or parallel traffic has green simultaneously.

🚂 Level Crossing Lights

Two red lights that are off when no train is approaching, and flash alternately when a train is approaching — you must stop. Some crossings also have a white flashing light to indicate it is safe to cross. Even if the barrier is up, you must wait until the red lights are off before crossing.

🌉 Bridge Lights

One or two red lights that are off when the bridge is closed (traffic may pass). When the bridge opens, the red light(s) activate — you must stop. With two red lights, they flash alternately.

🚌 Tram and Bus Lights (Nine-Eye)

Designed exclusively for public transport. Nine round lights in a square arrangement: top and bottom rows are white, the middle lamp is yellow, left and right lamps are red. Trams and buses using that lane must follow these lights.

Turn left allowed

Straight through allowed

Turn right allowed

Stop if reasonably possible

Stop

⚠️ Yellow and Red Flashing Lights

🚶 Pedestrian Lights

Most pedestrian lights have a red and a green pedestrian lamp. In some, the red is replaced by a yellow triangle with an exclamation mark.

Green

Pedestrians may cross.

Flashing Green

Pedestrians may cross, but the light will switch to red very soon.

Red

Pedestrians may no longer start crossing. Those already crossing should walk as quickly as possible.

Flashing Yellow

Pedestrians may cross, but must give way to other traffic. Crossing is at your own risk.

🛣️ Lane Lights (Matrix Signs)

Found above lanes on autowegen and motorways. Each display applies only to its own lane.

Speed Limit

Maximum speed for that lane. Not an advisory speed — it is mandatory. Also means the lane may be used. Sometimes combined with yellow flashing lamps in traffic jams.

Green Arrow

The lane may be used.

White Arrow

Advance notice of a red cross — clear this lane as soon as possible. Often combined with yellow flashing lights.

Red Cross

Using this lane is prohibited. It may only be used in a genuine emergency.

BUS / LIJNBUS

BUS — lane for bus drivers only. LIJNBUS — lane for public bus drivers only.

Matrix Sign F-9

End of all prohibitions indicated by electronic matrix signs. Standard rules apply from here.

⚠ Other Warning Signs on Matrix Signs

Warning signs can also appear on matrix signs to explain why the speed is reduced or a lane is closed.

J-33

Traffic jam warning

J-34

Accident warning
